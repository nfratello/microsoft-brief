Morning. Microsoft shipped a lot this week. I kept the part that matters to defense industry, and it fits in a few minutes.

For aerospace and defense organizations, this week's news is about running cloud and AI where connectivity is limited, and keeping systems current as threats speed up.

1 big thing

Running cloud and AI at the mission edge

What's new: Microsoft outlined how Azure Local disconnected operations, Foundry Local and Microsoft 365 Local can run cloud, AI and productivity workloads at the tactical edge without public cloud connectivity. It has also published implementation guides for selected NATO requirements up to Secret.

Why it matters for defense industry: Defense teams could run AI models and productivity tools on forward bases, ships and vehicles while keeping data and models under their own control.
Source: The Microsoft Cloud Blog · Nov 11
Share: LinkedIn · Email
 
Story 2

Patch faster as AI finds more flaws

What's new: Microsoft's IT team explained how it keeps software current as AI speeds up how fast flaws are found. Devices must restart and apply OS updates within two days, with Windows Autopatch, Hotpatch and Intune doing most of the work.

Why it matters for defense industry: Defense contractors protecting CUI face more vulnerabilities, found faster. Automated, deadline-driven patching could help shrink the risk window.
Source: Microsoft Inside Track · Nov 17
Share: LinkedIn · Email
 
Story 3

Copilot Cowork goes live in GCC

What's new: Copilot Cowork is now generally available in the Government Community Cloud, with GCC High to follow. It runs inside the GCC boundary on government-hosted models and pauses for user approval before it sends email or posts.

Why it matters for defense industry: GCC High is next, though timing may change. Defense industrial base teams can start preparing governance and licensing now.
Source: Microsoft Tech Community · Nov 18
Share: LinkedIn · Email
 
One move for Program & engineering · Defense Industry

Turn meeting notes into owned action items

Action items die in notes. A table with owners and dates keeps the program moving. Use only approved environments for controlled data.

Copy this prompt
From these design review notes (pasted below), list every action item with an owner and due date in a table. Flag anything blocked or missing an owner, and draft a short follow-up email to the team.

Where it works: Works in Copilot Chat: paste or attach your file. Why it works: it gives Copilot a goal, context, details and the data to use.

New in Copilot: Look for three tabs in the Copilot app: Chat for quick questions and drafts, Cowork for longer work you hand off, and Code for building small tools. A Work IQ switch decides whether answers use your work data or only the web.
