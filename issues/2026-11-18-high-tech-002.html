1 big thing

Microsoft and Intel expand their Azure deal

What's new: Microsoft and Intel signed a long-term agreement covering Azure infrastructure, including Intel Foundry's EMIB packaging for Azure first-party silicon and multi-year Xeon supply. The work cites up to 2.3x performance on Azure M-series v4 VMs with CXL memory and up to 2.5x faster SQL backups.

Why it matters for tech: Semiconductor and infrastructure firms get a signal on where cloud design is heading: custom packaging, CXL memory and confidential computing. Software firms could see faster, more secure VMs for data-heavy work.
Source: Microsoft Source · Nov 18
Share: LinkedIn · Email
 
Story 2

AI agents get their own managed Cloud PCs

What's new: Microsoft is giving AI digital workers their own managed Cloud PCs through Windows 365 for Agents, so they can use desktop apps that lack reliable APIs. Its internal toolkit already cut human touch by 75% and order cycle time from 25 to 10.3 hours.

Why it matters for tech: IT services and SaaS firms often support legacy apps with no clean API. Giving agents a governed Windows desktop could open those workflows to automation with the same controls used for people.
Source: Microsoft Inside Track · Nov 17
Share: LinkedIn · Email
 
Story 3

Same AI work, very different monthly bills

What's new: Microsoft-funded research with GigaOm compared the total cost of Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude Enterprise on the same tasks. On the modeled workloads, Copilot cost $30 a month for an everyday AI user, with broadly similar quality.

Why it matters for tech: Tech companies tend to be heavy AI users. Comparing total cost per task, not just seat price, can help keep AI spend predictable as usage grows.
Source: Microsoft Copilot Blog · Nov 16
