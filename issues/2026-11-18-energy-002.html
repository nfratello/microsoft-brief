1 big thing

An AI model to measure carbon removal

What's new: Terradot and Microsoft built TerraNova, an AI model that builds on Microsoft's Aurora weather model to predict how crushed rock reacts in farm soil. It aims to make carbon removal through enhanced rock weathering easier and cheaper to measure.

Why it matters for energy: Energy firms buying or developing carbon removal need credible, affordable measurement. Models like this could cut the cost of verification as the market grows.

Story 2

Frontier Firms see 4x returns on AI

What's new: New IDC research, commissioned by Microsoft, surveyed more than 5,200 leaders across 28 industries. Frontier Firms report an average 4x return on AI, versus 2.7x for slow adopters.

Why it matters for energy: Energy companies with many sites can learn from leaders that scale AI across business units and govern agents from the start.
Source: The Official Microsoft Blog · Nov 12
Share: LinkedIn · Email
 
Story 3

Same AI work, very different monthly bills

What's new: Microsoft-funded research with GigaOm compared the total cost of Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude Enterprise on the same tasks. On the modeled workloads, Copilot cost $30 a month for an everyday AI user, with broadly similar quality.

Why it matters for energy: Utilities and field-heavy energy firms can compare total cost per task before rolling AI out to large frontline workforces.
Source: Microsoft Copilot Blog · Nov 16
