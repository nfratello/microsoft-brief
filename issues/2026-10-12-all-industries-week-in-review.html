Copilot learns the law, an ERP Leader nod, and faster access shut-off
Archive edition: a look back at the public Microsoft announcements from this week, published November 2026 so new readers can catch up. Event details are as of that week.
Copilot picked up its first profession-specific skill pack this week, and Microsoft's ERP picked up an outside endorsement. Here's what matters.

Copilot
Copilot now speaks fluent contract

What's new: Microsoft made 20+ legal skills generally available in Copilot, with partner plugins such as Harvey, iManage, Docusign and Bloomberg Law, for redlining, drafting and obligation tracking in Word.

Why it matters: Legal and compliance teams can check contracts against their own playbooks without leaving Word, and every suggested edit still needs a person's approval.

Dynamics 365
An outside vote for Microsoft's AI-enabled ERP

What's new: IDC named Dynamics 365 a Leader in AI-enabled operational ERP, citing its MCP-first agent design and noting more than a third of customers go live in under six months.

Why it matters: Finance and supply chain leaders comparing ERP platforms get an independent view, plus a list of ready-made agents like Procurement and Account Reconciliation.

Security
Cut off risky devices in near real time

What's new: Entra added device-revocation and user-risk signals to continuous access evaluation (preview), a guided move from Connect Sync to Cloud Sync (GA) and access requests from inside ServiceNow (GA).

Why it matters: Access ends faster when a device falls out of compliance, and retiring on-premises sync servers gets easier.

Go deeper →  Microsoft Entra Blog
Azure
More AI capacity behind Copilot and Foundry

What's new: Microsoft's own Maia 200 chips now run production work including Copilot, and Maia 300, due in 2027, targets about 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems.

Why it matters: More efficient AI capacity shapes what agent-heavy workloads will cost you and how quickly they can scale.
