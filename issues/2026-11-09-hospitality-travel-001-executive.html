90 seconds: AI agents for your reservations center, AI agents that book travel, and the agents that can reach guest data.
The Microsoft Brief · Executive
Hospitality & Travel
Issue No. 1 · Monday, November 9, 2026 · ⏱ 90-second read
The big picture: AI agents are moving into the three places that matter most for airlines, hotels and cruise lines: the contact center, the booking path and the guest data behind both. Here's what changed, and what it's worth.
1 · CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE · COST
Add AI agents to your reservations center without replacing it
What changed: Microsoft Contact Center Intelligence is in public preview. Its customer-assist and quality-review agents plug into the contact center you already run.
What it's worth: Cost and risk: self-service that scales during disruption peaks, and every call reviewed for quality, without a platform migration.
CFO lens: Lower cost per contact without a rip-and-replace project.
Source: Microsoft Dynamics 365 blog, Oct 21, 2026 →
2 · DIGITAL · REVENUE
Can a traveler's AI agent actually book with you?
What changed: Microsoft Clarity now shows what AI agents do on your website and tests whether they can finish key tasks, starting with commerce and travel.
What it's worth: Revenue: as travelers hand booking to AI assistants, you can see, and fix, the bookings you would otherwise lose without knowing.
CMO lens: Make sure AI assistants can find, compare and book your rooms, fares and sailings.
Source: Microsoft Clarity blog, Nov 4, 2026 →
3 · SECURITY · RISK
See every AI agent that can reach guest data
What changed: Microsoft Defender can now find AI agents running on employee devices, and the tools they connect to. It is generally available.
What it's worth: Risk: travel brands hold payment cards, passports and loyalty data. Knowing which agents can reach them lowers breach and compliance exposure.
CISO lens: Start with an inventory of AI agents before setting policy for them.
Source: Microsoft Tech Community, Oct 29, 2026 →
